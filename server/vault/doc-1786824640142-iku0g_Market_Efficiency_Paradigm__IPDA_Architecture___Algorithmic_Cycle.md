# Market Efficiency Paradigm: IPDA Architecture & Algorithmic Cycle

## Executive Summary
The provided source material outlines the foundational components of the Interbank Price Delivery Algorithm (IPDA) architecture, specifically detailing the four core phases of the Algorithmic Cycle of Price Delivery: Consolidation, Expansion, Retracement, and Reversal.

This framework conceptualizes market price movements as an engineered, algorithmic sequence driven by market makers rather than random fluctuations. The model establishes how price oscillates around an equilibrium baseline, rapidly reprices through explosive expansion, rebalances inefficiency via retracement, and targets external liquidity pools prior to reversing direction.

## Key Takeaways
- Market price moves through a four-stage algorithmic delivery cycle: Consolidation, Expansion, Retracement, and Reversal.
- Consolidation centers strictly on an equilibrium price point defined as the 50% level of the established range.
- Expansion represents institutional displacement and directional intent to reprice the market.
- Retracement serves to rebalance inefficiently delivered price action within the newly expanded range.
- Reversal occurs following the breach of major liquidity pools (old highs and lows), prompting aggressive movement in the opposing direction.

### IPDA Architecture & Algorithmic Price Cycle

The Interbank Price Delivery Algorithm (IPDA) operates in a continuous four-stage cycle:

1. **Consolidation**:
   - Characterized by range-bound price action.
   - Price oscillates symmetrically around an equilibrium point ($50\%$ of the range).
   - Accumulates orders and establishes boundaries for future liquidity pools.

2. **Expansion**:
   - Features sudden, explosive displacement.
   - Breaks outside the consolidation boundary.
   - Signals immediate institutional and market maker repricing intent.

3. **Retracement**:
   - Order flow counter-trend movement back inside the newly created price range.
   - Primary function is to rebalance price inefficiencies created during the rapid expansion phase.

4. **Reversal**:
   - Occurs after price sweeps or breaches key liquidity pools (previous significant highs or lows).
   - Characterized by aggressive, high-momentum movement in the opposite direction.

**Key Terms:**
- **Consolidation**: A state where price oscillates within a defined range around a 50% equilibrium level.
- **Expansion**: A sudden, explosive displacement away from equilibrium indicating market maker repricing intent.
- **Retracement**: Order flow moving back inside a newly formed range to rebalance inefficiently delivered price.
- **Reversal**: An aggressive shift in price direction triggered after breaching key liquidity pools (old highs or lows).
**Formulas/Rules:**
- **Equilibrium Formula**: `\text{Equilibrium} = \frac{\text{Range High} + \text{Range Low}}{2} = 50\% \text{ Level}` — Calculates the central balance point around which consolidation occurs.
- **Algorithmic Delivery Sequence**: `\text{Consolidation} \rightarrow \text{Expansion} \rightarrow \text{Retracement} \rightarrow \text{Reversal / Expansion}` — The chronological progression of institutional price delivery stages.
